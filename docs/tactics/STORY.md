# Conflict direction: compliance, extraction and self-government

Recorded from the user's latest direction. This supersedes the earlier decision to defer all story discussion. It establishes the campaign brief; the current factory prototype does not yet implement surveillance, pacification, brainwashing, factions, rescue facilities or endings.

## Confirmed direction

The state prefers a compliant workforce to meeting people's needs. Surveillance, pacification and fictional brainwashing are central to its control. Keep the anthropomorphic cast and Soviet industrial visual language, with the authoritarian absurdity and institutional tropes the user associates with Zoo City 2000.

The mercenaries are morally compromised. Their likely corporate sponsors want privileged access to a local resource, so defeating the local rulers can simply replace one form of domination with another. The corporations must remain interested parties, not a neutral source of help.

The hardest ending must deny both the despots and the sponsoring corporations their intended victory and leave the population able to govern itself. It should require building the conditions for self-government, rather than handing power to the mercenaries or pressing a final independence button.

## Working resource premise, not yet final

The user favors exploring an endemic botanical resource over the conventional mineral deposit. It may be connected to the fictional brainwashing system, grow only in this region, and resist synthesis. Its species, name, biological explanation and uses are undecided. Do not assign real pharmacology or a real-world brainwashing procedure to it.

A useful fictional option is a plant whose useful product depends on an inseparable local ecological partnership. Industrial cultivation and processing could create the same factories, transport links and guarded facilities already needed by the tactical game. This is a proposal, not a settled explanation for why it cannot be synthesized.

Avoid making destruction of the plant the only ethical choice. Communities may depend on its legitimate uses or cultivation. The conflict can concern who controls production and whether coercive processing continues, preserving a reason to protect workers, local knowledge and the habitat.

## Proposed campaign systems

Surveillance should alter observable tactical conditions: cameras, records, watch posts and communication links affect suspicion, identification and reinforcement response. Make their functions legible so reconnaissance and sabotage offer alternatives to simply shooting every guard.

Pacification infrastructure can overlap with public services. Disabling a coercive installation may interrupt useful work unless the player secures an alternative. Present the practical consequence and a repair or takeover route; do not make civilians suffer automatically merely to make the story dark.

Fictional brainwashing removes agency without making its victims inherently evil. Rescue and recovery can matter alongside combat. Do not make an affected character's beliefs a substitute for deciding whether they pose an immediate threat. Retain consequences for mercenary violence and for choices made on behalf of sponsors.

Corporate support can offer equipment, intelligence and supplies in exchange for concessions. These bargains should make the sponsor-aligned route easier and the independent route materially harder, without hiding the tradeoff until the ending.

Local people need independent goals and organized capacity. Workers, growers and other community groups should disagree, negotiate and sometimes refuse the mercenaries. Their role is not to gratefully receive sovereignty from foreign gunmen.

## Proposed ending structure

- Regime continuity: the old coercive order survives or reconstitutes itself.
- Corporate victory: the old rulers fall, but outside sponsors secure control of the resource and the new authorities.
- Failed liberation: coercive leaders fall without sufficient institutions or supplies to prevent renewed dependency or collapse.
- Self-government: local institutions control essential services and resource decisions, coercive infrastructure cannot be reactivated by either patron, and no mercenary or sponsor retains an effective veto.

The self-government route is explicitly the hardest intended success. Proposed requirements are a viable local coalition, independent supplies, protection or restoration of essential services, and prevention of a successor monopoly over the coercive system and resource. These should become visible campaign objectives with several ways to satisfy them, not an opaque collection of perfect-choice flags. Breaking sponsor dependence should cost resources and tactical opportunities. It need not require a flawless casualty-free run or the completion of every optional mission.

Do not equate self-government with a universally happy population or permanent absence of conflict. The outcome is the ability to make and contest local decisions without the regime, corporations or mercenaries dictating them.

## Capture and rescue continuity

On defeat, stabilized mercenaries are captured; those still bleeding die. Training is permanent and cannot be respecced. Preserving a captured mercenary's identity, skills and history gives the planned mid-to-late-game rescue facility a meaningful payoff.

Implemented now: distinct captured/dead results and an independent captured-roster snapshot in the defeated local-map state. Not implemented yet: campaign continuation after a wipe, a playable rescue facility, detainee transfer rules, or campaign persistence across browser reloads. Those systems are prerequisites for completing a later rescue, not features to imply are already playable. Replacement recruitment exists since 2026-09-18 as the hiring hall (ECONOMY.md, "Mercenary contracts"): hired mercs on day, week or month contracts beside the four comrades; it does not yet continue a campaign after a wipe.

## Decisions to make when campaign work begins

1. Is the resource itself useful for ordinary purposes, with only a particular fictional processing method enabling coercion?
2. What kinds of local institutions must the player help keep functioning for self-government to be viable?
3. How does a campaign continue after a total squad defeat: reserve mercenaries, new recruits, or another established team?
4. Are captured mercenaries' equipment and injuries recoverable along with their skills, and can prisoners be moved between facilities?
